# 02 — Research Methodology

## Research stance
Treat each instrument and tradition as a situated cultural practice, not simply an object taxonomy. Document variation, contested terminology, community context and limits of available evidence.

## Evidence hierarchy
Prioritise:
1. Primary documentation from practitioners, makers, community organisations and relevant cultural institutions.
2. Peer-reviewed scholarship, academic books and specialist catalogues.
3. Reputable museums, archives and public cultural institutions.
4. Recorded interviews and documentary sources with identifiable provenance.
5. General reference sources only for orientation, never as the sole support for sensitive or disputed claims.

A source's institutional status does not automatically validate every claim. Record the exact page, passage, object record or timestamp used.

## Research workflow
1. Confirm the approved list of traditions and scope.
2. Define the geographic and cultural boundaries carefully; avoid treating state borders as cultural boundaries.
3. Build a terminology register including local names, transliterations, alternate spellings and language/script.
4. Identify instruments through multiple sources.
5. Verify classification, construction, materials, playing technique and musical role separately.
6. Research makers and musicians using attributable first-person or institutional documentation.
7. Document craft processes stage by stage, distinguishing common practice from individual workshop practice.
8. Verify media ownership, licence, permitted use and attribution independently of factual research.
9. Record disagreements and gaps rather than forcing a single answer.
10. Conduct review with relevant practitioners or subject specialists where feasible.

## Claim-level provenance
Every non-trivial claim should be linked to one or more source IDs. A source record should include author/institution, title, URL, publication date if known, access date, source type, relevant section/page/timestamp, and the claims it supports.

## Confidence labels
- `verified`: directly supported by a credible, relevant source.
- `corroborated`: supported by multiple independent sources.
- `partial`: only part of the statement is supported.
- `disputed`: credible sources conflict.
- `unverified`: not yet adequately supported.
- `not_applicable`: field genuinely does not apply.
- `unknown`: not known; do not infer.

## Interview ethics
Obtain informed consent for recording, photography, quotations and publication. Explain intended audience and distribution. Confirm preferred name, spelling, pronouns where relevant, role description and attribution. Offer review of quotations and sensitive contextual material. Do not assume that public visibility means permission to reuse.

## Cultural representation
Avoid exoticising language, claims of timelessness, simplistic origin stories and statements that a tradition is uniform. Name communities and practitioners accurately where they consent. Respect restrictions around sacred, private or community-controlled knowledge.
