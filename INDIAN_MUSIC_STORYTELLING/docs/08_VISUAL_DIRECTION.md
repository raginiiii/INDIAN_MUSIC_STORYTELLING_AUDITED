# 08 — Visual Direction

## Approved direction: The Living Archive, in Motion

The website should feel like an editorial archive that is alive rather than a museum catalogue frozen behind glass. Its visual language combines the clarity and restraint of contemporary editorial design with the tactile character of archival photography, material studies, workshop documentation and carefully composed typography. Fraunces or Instrument Serif should provide expressive, humanist display moments, balanced by a highly legible sans-serif for navigation, metadata and longer reading. Warm paper-like neutrals, deep ink tones and restrained earthy accents create a calm foundation; colour should emerge from the content and imagery rather than become a decorative theme. Sound and motion should make discovery active: a selected instrument reveals its anatomy, a craft sequence unfolds through scroll or direct selection, and audio becomes available through deliberate user action. The result should be immersive without being cluttered, experimental without sacrificing clarity, and culturally grounded without relying on generic “ethnic” visual clichés.

## Design interpretation
- Editorial and accessible: clear hierarchy, generous whitespace and readable line lengths.
- Living archive: source-aware imagery, captions, material details and human stories.
- Sound in motion: meaningful audio controls, subtle waveform states and purposeful transitions.
- Progressive discovery: reveal complexity in layers.
- Documentary integrity: distinguish archival evidence, contemporary photography, illustration and generated decorative texture.

## Visual guardrails
Do not use generic ornamental motifs, pseudo-script, unverified historical imagery, decorative “tribal” patterns or stock imagery presented as a specific tradition. Do not apply a uniform sepia filter to all cultural material. Preserve the context and dignity of photographed people and workshops.
